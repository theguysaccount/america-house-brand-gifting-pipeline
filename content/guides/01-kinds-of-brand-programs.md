Brands use these words loosely, and one page often mixes two of them. This is what each word usually means, so you know what you are applying for before you fill in a form.

## Ambassador programs

An ongoing relationship. The brand picks people to represent it for months, not for one post.

- **What you usually get:** a personal discount code to share, free or discounted products, early access to launches, and sometimes commission on sales made with your code.
- **What it usually asks for:** regular posts, tagging the brand, and following its content guidelines.
- **Watch for:** programs that are only a discount code. If the "program" is that you buy the products at 30% off and post about them, the brand is selling to you, not partnering with you.

## Affiliate programs

A commission arrangement. You get a tracked link or code, and you earn a percentage of the sales it brings in.

- **What you usually get:** a commission on each sale, usually paid monthly once you pass a minimum balance. On the {commission_n} pages in this directory that state a rate, the median is {commission_median}% (as of {asof}).
- **What it usually asks for:** nothing fixed. You earn when people buy, and not otherwise.
- **Watch for:** the cookie window (how long after a click a sale still counts for you), the payout minimum, and whether your own purchases count.

Affiliate programs are the easiest kind to join. Many approve everyone who applies. [Browse affiliate programs]({base}/type/affiliate/)

## Creator and influencer programs

The brand is looking for people who make content: sponsored posts, reviews, or UGC (photos and videos the brand uses on its own channels and ads).

- **What you usually get:** free products, a fee per piece of content, or both.
- **What it usually asks for:** links to your profiles, your follower counts, examples of your work, and your rates.
- **Watch for:** usage rights. If the brand wants to run your video as a paid ad, that is worth more than a post on your own account. Get the terms in writing.

[Browse creator programs]({base}/type/creator/)

## PR lists and gifting

The brand sends products to people it hopes will talk about them, with no payment and usually no obligation to post.

- **What you usually get:** products, sometimes ahead of a launch.
- **What it usually asks for:** your shipping address and profiles. Some brands ask for a post in return; then it is a gifted collaboration and you should treat it like any other deal.
- **Watch for:** few brands publish an open PR-list form. Most gifting happens through ambassador and creator programs. The list of [programs whose pages mention free products]({base}/lists/free-products/) is the closest thing to a public PR list.

## Whatever the label

- Read the brand's own terms. The label on the page tells you less than the terms do.
- Applying should be free. See [how to spot a fake ambassador offer]({base}/guides/spot-a-fake-brand-ambassador-offer/).
- If you receive products, payment or commission, you have to say so when you post. See [how to disclose]({base}/guides/disclose-gifted-products-and-affiliate-links/).

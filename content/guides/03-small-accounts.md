You do not need a large following to work with brands. Small accounts are often what small brands want: your followers know you, and your recommendation reads like one.

## Where small accounts get accepted

- **Affiliate programs.** Most have no follower requirement. You get a link, and you are paid when someone buys. See [affiliate programs]({base}/type/affiliate/).
- **Programs that say so.** As of {asof}, {nomin} program pages in this directory state that there is no follower minimum, and others name a low one. See [programs open to small accounts]({base}/lists/no-follower-minimum/).
- **UGC work.** Brands that buy photos and videos for their own channels care about the content, not your audience. Look under [creator programs]({base}/type/creator/) for pages that mention UGC.
- **Small and local brands.** A brand with a few thousand followers of its own is not waiting for a celebrity.

## What a small account should show instead of numbers

- **A clear subject.** "Trail running in the Pacific Northwest" is easier to say yes to than "lifestyle".
- **Engagement you can point to.** Comments and saves on your posts matter more than the follower count.
- **Proof you can make the content.** Two or three posts about products you already own, shot the way you would shoot for a brand.
- **Reliability.** Reply quickly, and deliver what you said you would on the date you said.

## What to expect

- Products and commission are much more common than fees at this size. That is normal, and it is still a deal: [disclose it]({base}/guides/disclose-gifted-products-and-affiliate-links/).
- Do not pay to take part. A program that asks a small creator to buy products first is a sale, not a partnership. See [how to spot a fake ambassador offer]({base}/guides/spot-a-fake-brand-ambassador-offer/).
- Do not buy followers to reach a minimum. Brands check, and it ends the relationship.

[Search the directory]({base}/#browse) and use the "The page mentions" filter to find programs that welcome small accounts.

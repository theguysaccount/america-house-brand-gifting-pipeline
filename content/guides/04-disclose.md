If a brand gives you anything of value and you post about it, your audience has a right to know. In the United States that is a legal requirement enforced by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC). This page summarizes the FTC's own guidance. It is general information, not legal advice. The official source is the FTC's [guidance for influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/influencers).

## When you have to disclose

Disclose whenever you have what the FTC calls a "material connection" to a brand. That includes:

- being paid;
- receiving free or discounted products, even with no obligation to post;
- earning commission from an affiliate link or code;
- a business or family relationship with the brand.

Being an ambassador with a discount code counts. So does a gifted product you were not asked to post about, if you then post about it.

## How to disclose

The FTC's guidance comes down to this: make it hard to miss, and use plain words.

- **Put it with the endorsement itself.** Not only in your bio, not at the end of a long caption behind "more", and not buried in a string of hashtags.
- **In a video, say it and show it.** The FTC says the disclosure should be in the video, not just in the description. In a live stream, repeat it now and then for people who join late.
- **In a photo or story, put it on the image** where people will see it and have time to read it.
- **Use simple wording.** "Ad", "Sponsored", "#ad", "Thanks to Acme for the free product", or "I earn a commission if you buy through this link" all work.
- **Avoid vague tags.** The FTC specifically warns against terms like "sp", "spon" and "collab", and against stand-alone words like "thanks" or "ambassador", because people do not understand them.
- **Do not rely only on a platform's built-in tool.** The FTC says you can use a "paid partnership" label, but you are still responsible for a disclosure people will notice.
- **Disclose in the language of the post.**

## What you cannot say

- You cannot review a product you have not tried.
- If you were paid to talk about a product and thought it was bad, you cannot say it was great.
- You cannot make claims about a product that the brand could not prove, for example that it treats a health condition.

## Outside the United States

The FTC says U.S. law applies if it is reasonably foreseeable that your post will affect U.S. consumers, wherever you live. Other countries have their own rules, and they are similar in spirit: say clearly when something is an ad. In the UK, see the Advertising Standards Authority's guidance for influencers. In Canada, see Ad Standards' influencer disclosure guidelines. In Australia, see the AANA Code of Ethics and the ACCC's guidance.

## A simple habit

Before you post about any brand, ask one question: did I get anything from them? If yes, say so in the first line.

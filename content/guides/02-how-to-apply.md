Most brand applications ask the same handful of questions. Prepare the answers once, keep them in a note, and each application takes two minutes.

## Before you apply

1. **Pick brands you would buy anyway.** Brands can tell. An application that mentions the product you already use beats a generic one.
2. **Make your profile ready to be looked at.** Public account, a clear bio that says what you post about, and recent posts. A reviewer will spend about thirty seconds on it.
3. **Check where the brand ships.** Many programs are limited to one country. Cards in this directory show where a store is based and ships when the store publishes that.
4. **Read the program page to the end.** Requirements such as a follower minimum, a posting schedule or exclusivity are usually near the bottom.

## What the form will ask

Have these ready to paste:

- Your name, email and the country you live in.
- Links to your profiles, with your handle spelled exactly.
- Follower counts. Use today's real numbers. Brands check, and a rounded-up number ends the conversation.
- What you post about and who watches it, in one or two sentences.
- Two or three links to posts that show your style, ideally ones about a product.
- Why this brand. One specific sentence is enough: the product you use and what you would make with it.
- A shipping address, if products are involved.

Never put a password, a bank login or an ID number in an application form. A real brand does not need them to consider you.

## A short pitch that works

When the form has a free-text box, three sentences cover it:

> I'm Sam, I post weekday breakfast recipes on TikTok and Instagram for about 4,000 people, mostly students. I've used your oat granola in videos since spring. I'd make three recipe videos a month with it and send you the files for your own channels.

It says who you are, shows you know the product, and names what the brand gets.

## After you apply

- **Keep a list.** Brand, date, the page you applied on, and what you sent. A spreadsheet or a note is enough.
- **Expect silence from most.** Affiliate programs often approve within days. Ambassador and creator programs can take weeks, and many only reply to people they accept.
- **Follow up once.** If the page gives a contact and two or three weeks have passed, one short, polite message is fine. More than one is not.
- **Apply to several.** Ten good applications beat one perfect one.

## When a brand says yes

- Get the terms in writing: what you receive, what you post, by when, and how the brand may reuse your content.
- Ask how and when commission is paid, and what the minimum payout is.

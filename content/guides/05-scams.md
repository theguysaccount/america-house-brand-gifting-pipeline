Real brands recruit ambassadors. So do people pretending to be brands, and brands whose "ambassador program" is a way to sell you products. These are the signs to look for.

## Warning signs

- **You are asked to pay.** A joining fee, a "starter kit", a deposit, or "just cover shipping" for your free products. Real programs do not charge people to apply or to join.
- **You must buy first.** "You've been selected! Use this code for 50% off your first order to activate your ambassador status." That is a discount, not a partnership. It may be legal, but you are the customer.
- **The message came to you, from an account you cannot verify.** A DM or comment from "the collab team" asking you to message a second account is a common pattern. Brands that recruit this way exist, but so do impersonators.
- **The link is not the brand's own website.** Check the address letter by letter. Look for extra words, swapped letters and unusual endings.
- **They want sensitive details early.** Bank logins, card numbers, passwords, verification codes or a photo of your ID. An application needs your handle and an email address, not these.
- **They send you money and ask for some of it back.** A check or transfer that is "too much by mistake" is a classic overpayment scam. The payment bounces later, and the money you sent back is gone.
- **You have to decide today.** Pressure is a tactic. A real program will still be there next week.

## How to check an offer

1. **Go to the brand's website yourself.** Type the address or search for it. Do not use the link in the message.
2. **Look for the program page there.** Real programs are usually linked in the site's footer or menu. Every listing in [this directory]({base}/#browse) is a page on the brand's own site.
3. **Compare the contact details.** The email domain in the message should match the brand's website.
4. **Ask the brand through its official contact page** whether the account that messaged you is theirs.
5. **Search the brand name with "ambassador" and "scam".** Other people's experiences show up fast.

## About "discount ambassador" programs

Some real brands run programs where the only benefit is a discount on your own purchases, sometimes with commission on top. That is not fraud, but understand what it is before you buy: a sales promotion. The crawler behind this directory leaves out pages that ask for a fee or a kit purchase to join, as far as it can tell from the page. If you find one here, please [tell us]({base}/methodology/#for-brands).

## If you already paid or shared details

- **Contact your bank or card provider straight away** and ask about reversing the payment.
- **Change the passwords** of any account whose details you shared, and turn on two-step verification.
- **Report it.** In the United States, report fraud to the FTC at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). Also report the account on the platform where you were contacted.
